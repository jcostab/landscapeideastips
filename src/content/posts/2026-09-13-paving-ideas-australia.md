Paving transforms soft, muddy, or unusable ground into functional hardscape. But choosing the right paving type for your application matters enormously - what works brilliantly for a garden path performs poorly as a driveway, and vice versa.

Here's your comprehensive guide to paving options for Australian homes, covering paths, patios, and driveways with honest assessments of what works where.

## Paving Types Explained

### Concrete Pavers

**What they are:** Pre-cast concrete units in standardised sizes and shapes.

**Sizes:** 
- Common: 400x400mm, 300x300mm, 230x115mm (brick-sized)
- Formats: Square, rectangular, brick-style, interlocking

**Finishes:**
- Smooth
- Textured/patterned surface
- Exposed aggregate face
- Tumbled/aged effect

**Advantages:**
- Wide availability and choice
- Mid-range price point
- Consistent sizing (easier installation)
- DIY-friendly for competent homeowners
- Easy to replace individual units if damaged
- Colour range available (though limited vs natural stone)

**Considerations:**
- Colour can fade over time (quality varies)
- Less premium appearance than natural stone
- Can shift if base isn't properly prepared
- Joints need occasional weed control

**Best for:** Budget to mid-range patios, paths, courtyards, driveways (if rated appropriately)

**Cost:** $40-80 per m² materials, $80-150 per m² installed

### Natural Stone Pavers

**Stone types:**
- **Bluestone** - classic Victorian, dark grey, very durable, premium price
- **Sandstone** - warm tones (cream to brown), softer stone, mid-high price
- **Limestone** - light colours, coastal aesthetic, mid price
- **Granite** - extremely hard, varied colours, premium price
- **Slate** - dark grey/black, textured surface, mid-high price

**Advantages:**
- Premium natural appearance
- Each piece unique
- Colour won't fade (natural stone)
- Excellent durability (hard stones)
- Adds property value
- Suits traditional and contemporary designs

**Considerations:**
- Higher cost than concrete alternatives
- Professional installation recommended (irregular sizes, heavy)
- Some stones prone to staining (limestone, sandstone)
- Can be slippery when wet (choose honed not polished)
- Colour/thickness variation between batches

**Best for:** High-end patios, feature areas, formal gardens, matching heritage architecture, properties wanting natural materials

**Cost:** $100-250+ per m² materials, $180-400+ per m² installed

### Crazy Paving

**What it is:** Irregular-shaped natural stone pieces fitted together like a jigsaw.

**Stone types:**
- Bluestone crazy pave (most common)
- Sandstone crazy pave
- Slate crazy pave

**Advantages:**
- Distinctive natural appearance
- Uses offcuts (more economical than cut stone)
- Flexible for curved or irregular areas
- Traditional Australian aesthetic
- Durable (stone is still stone)

**Considerations:**
- Uneven surface (not ideal for furniture)
- Labour-intensive installation (professional job)
- Can look dated if not done well
- Trip hazard risk if pieces vary too much in height
- Not suitable for driveways (unstable under vehicle loads)

**Best for:** Garden paths, informal courtyards, period-appropriate renovations, areas wanting rustic charm, curved paths

**Cost:** $120-200 per m² materials, $200-350 per m² installed

**Peninsula suitability:** Crazy paving works beautifully for garden paths in established gardens but has fallen out of favour for entertaining areas - too uneven for outdoor furniture.

### Exposed Aggregate Concrete

**What it is:** Concrete surface with aggregate (stones) exposed and visible on top layer.

**Process:** Concrete poured, surface treated to remove top cement layer, revealing aggregate below.

**Aggregate options:**
- Local river stones
- Coloured gravels
- White quartz
- Black basalt
- Mixed blends

**Advantages:**
- Cost-effective for large areas
- Durable (monolithic surface)
- Non-slip texture
- Modern contemporary appearance
- Good for driveways (rated for vehicle loads)
- Minimal joints (less weed issues)

**Considerations:**
- Professional installation required
- Difficult to repair if damaged (patching shows)
- Hot underfoot in summer
- Large expanses can look monotonous
- Permanent (hard to modify later)

**Best for:** Driveways, large patios, modern homes, pool surrounds, commercial-looking residential areas, sloped sites (can follow contours)

**Cost:** $100-180 per m² installed (varies with aggregate choice)

### Permeable/Porous Paving

**What it is:** Paving that allows water to drain through rather than running off.

**Types:**
- **Permeable pavers** - concrete units with wide joints and drainage layer below
- **Porous pavers** - porous concrete units themselves allow water through
- **Grass pavers** - grid structure filled with grass or gravel
- **Gravel with grid reinforcement** - structural grid prevents gravel migration

**Advantages:**
- Reduces stormwater runoff
- Council-preferred (or required) for some applications
- Good for environmental sustainability
- Reduces puddles and surface water
- Can satisfy planning requirements for permeability

**Considerations:**
- Requires proper base design (drainage layer critical)
- Can clog over time (requires maintenance)
- Not suitable for all soil types (clay needs careful design)
- Professional installation recommended
- Slightly higher cost than standard paving

**Best for:** Driveways (meeting council permeability requirements), large paved areas, properties with drainage concerns, environmentally-conscious homeowners

**Cost:** $120-220 per m² installed (depending on system)

### Clay Brick Pavers

**What they are:** Fired clay bricks designed for paving (thinner than house bricks).

**Advantages:**
- Classic appearance
- Colour won't fade (fired clay)
- Traditional Australian aesthetic
- Durable
- Suitable for paths, patios, driveways
- Wide range of laying patterns (herringbone, stretcher bond, basketweave)

**Considerations:**
- Mid to high price
- Can be slippery when wet (smooth face)
- Moss/algae growth in shaded damp areas
- Professional installation recommended for driveways
- Limited colour range (reds, browns, creams)

**Best for:** Heritage properties, formal gardens, matching existing brick architecture, traditional designs, properties wanting classic Australian look

**Cost:** $70-120 per m² materials, $150-250 per m² installed

### Resin-Bound Paving

**What it is:** Resin mixed with aggregate, applied as seamless surface.

**Advantages:**
- Smooth, seamless finish
- Permeable (allows drainage)
- Contemporary appearance
- Wide colour/aggregate options
- Comfortable to walk on

**Considerations:**
- Requires professional installation
- UV exposure can cause colour change over time
- Can be damaged by vehicle turning (sharp steering on spot)
- Repairs difficult to blend seamlessly
- Newer technology (less long-term data)

**Best for:** Modern properties, feature paths, permeable driveway alternative, pool surrounds

**Cost:** $150-280 per m² installed

### Loose Materials (Gravel, Crushed Rock)

**What they are:** Non-fixed paving materials.

**Types:**
- **Pea gravel** - rounded small stones, comfortable underfoot
- **Crushed rock** - angular rock fragments, compacts better
- **Decomposed granite** - fine crushed granite, compacts firm
- **Bluestone screenings** - fine bluestone dust/chips

**Advantages:**
- Lowest cost option
- Easy DIY installation
- Good drainage
- Suits cottage gardens and informal settings
- Easy to top up or modify

**Considerations:**
- Migrates (spreads beyond edged area)
- Weeds can establish
- Not suitable for driveways (spreads under tyres)
- Uncomfortable in bare feet (except finer materials)
- Requires edging to contain
- Regular top-ups needed (compacts and spreads)

**Best for:** Informal garden paths, between stepping stones, cottage gardens, temporary solutions, budget projects

**Cost:** $30-80 per m² materials + edging, DIY installation

## Application-Specific Recommendations

### Garden Paths

**Light use (access to garden beds):**
- Crazy paving
- Stepping stones in gravel
- Loose materials with edging
- Budget concrete pavers

**Heavy use (main route from house to garden):**
- Standard concrete pavers
- Natural stone pavers
- Clay brick pavers
- Exposed aggregate (if path is wide/straight)

**Priorities:** Stable surface, weather resistance, fits garden aesthetic, affordable

### Patios and Entertaining Areas

**Budget:**
- Concrete pavers (textured finish)
- Exposed aggregate
- Basic limestone pavers

**Mid-range:**
- Quality concrete pavers (tumbled/aged styles)
- Sandstone pavers
- Clay brick pavers

**Premium:**
- Bluestone pavers
- Granite pavers
- Limestone (high grade)

**Priorities:** Level surface (for furniture), attractive appearance, suits house style, comfortable barefoot

For more detailed patio design guidance, see our guide on [patio layouts that work](/blog/2026-09-13-patio-ideas-australia).

### Driveways

**Residential driveways need:**
- Load-bearing capacity (vehicle weight)
- Surface stability (no shifting under tyres)
- All-weather performance
- Minimal maintenance

**Best options:**
- **Exposed aggregate concrete** - most popular, good value, durable
- **Concrete pavers** (driveway-rated thickness) - attractive, repairable
- **Clay brick pavers** - traditional, durable
- **Permeable paving** - if council requires stormwater management
- **Plain concrete** - most economical, less attractive

**Avoid for driveways:**
- Crazy paving (unstable under loads)
- Loose materials (spreads and creates mess)
- Thin pavers not rated for vehicles

### Pool Surrounds

**Requirements:**
- Non-slip surface (wet foot traffic)
- Heat tolerance (summer sun, barefoot)
- Chemical resistance (chlorine/salt)
- Level and stable

**Best options:**
- **Natural stone pavers** (honed not polished) - premium, cooler underfoot
- **Concrete pavers** (textured) - practical, cost-effective
- **Exposed aggregate** (smooth aggregate) - good drainage
- **Resin-bound** - seamless, no trip edges

**Avoid:**
- Smooth surfaces (slippery when wet)
- Dark colours (too hot in summer)
- Loose materials (tracked into pool)

### Courtyard and Side Access

**Narrow side access:**
- Smaller pavers work better (easier to handle in tight space)
- Straight-edged pavers simplify installation
- Consider future access needs (utilities, maintenance)

**Courtyards:**
- Any paving type suitable
- Choose based on design aesthetic and budget
- Consider whether furniture or outdoor dining planned

## Installation Considerations

### Base Preparation (Critical)

Paving performance depends on base quality:

**Standard base (paths, patios):**
1. Excavate to depth (typically 150-200mm total)
2. Compact subgrade
3. 75-100mm roadbase or crushed rock, compacted
4. 30-50mm bedding sand (for pavers)
5. Pavers installed and compacted

**Driveway base:**
- Deeper excavation (200-250mm+)
- Thicker roadbase layer (150mm+)
- Better compaction (mechanical compactor)
- Edge restraint essential

**Clay soil (Peninsula properties):**
- Clay movement requires more robust base
- Better drainage crucial
- Professional advice recommended for driveways

### Edge Restraint

Pavers need edge restraint to prevent spreading:
- Concrete edge beam (most permanent)
- Steel edge restraint (good for curves)
- Treated timber edge (garden beds)
- Existing structures (house, retaining walls)

Never skip edge restraint - pavers without edges spread and collapse within months.

### Drainage and Falls

All paving needs slight slope for drainage:
- Minimum 1:100 fall (1cm per metre)
- Slope away from buildings
- Consider where water will drain to
- Poor drainage = puddles, moss, premature failure

### DIY vs Professional

**DIY suitable for:**
- Small paths (under 10m²)
- Simple rectangular layouts
- Standard pavers on level ground
- Garden paths (non-critical)
- Homeowners with patience and research

**Professional recommended for:**
- Patios (large area, need quality finish)
- Driveways (structural requirements)
- Complex layouts or curves
- Sloped sites
- Clay soils
- Natural stone or irregular materials
- When longevity and appearance matter

Professional paving includes proper base, compaction, drainage, edge restraint, and quality installation. The cost difference often justifies itself in longevity alone.

## Maintenance Requirements

### Sealed Paving

Some paving benefits from sealing:
- **Natural stone** - reduces staining, enhances colour
- **Concrete pavers** - improves weather resistance

**Re-sealing:** Every 3-5 years depending on traffic

### Weed Control

Pavers with joints:
- Hand-pull weeds when small
- Boiling water treatment
- Organic sprays (vinegar-based)
- Chemical weed killer (if comfortable)
- Polymeric sand in joints (reduces weed growth)

### Cleaning

- Regular sweeping removes debris
- Pressure washing annually (careful with softer stones)
- Moss/algae treatment in shade or damp areas
- Stain treatment as needed

### Relevelling

Over time, pavers can sink or shift:
- Lift affected pavers
- Add/adjust bedding sand
- Re-lay and compact

## Common Paving Mistakes

### 1. Inadequate Base

Thin or poorly compacted base causes premature failure. Never skimp on base preparation.

### 2. Poor Drainage

Flat paving (no fall) leads to puddles, moss, and water damage. Always install with slight slope.

### 3. No Edge Restraint

Pavers without edges spread outward. This isn't optional.

### 4. Wrong Paver Type for Application

Pathway pavers aren't suitable for driveways. Match paver specifications to intended use.

### 5. DIY Beyond Skill Level

Large or complex paving projects exceed most homeowner capabilities. Professional results justify the cost.

## Environmental Considerations

### Permeable Paving Benefits

Traditional paving creates impermeable surfaces:
- Increases stormwater runoff
- Contributes to local flooding
- Reduces groundwater recharge

Permeable paving helps:
- Reduces pressure on stormwater systems
- Improves local water management
- May be council-preferred or required

### Heat Island Effect

Large paved areas increase local temperatures:
- Dark paving absorbs heat
- Light-coloured paving reflects heat but still radiates
- Consider shade trees or structures near large paved areas
- Break up large expanses with planted areas

## Final Thoughts

Choosing the right paving for your application comes down to:

- **Function** - What will the surface be used for?
- **Budget** - What can you realistically afford?
- **Aesthetic** - What suits your property style?
- **Longevity** - How permanent is this installation?
- **DIY vs professional** - What's your capability and time?

**For garden paths:** Crazy paving, standard concrete pavers, or loose materials suit most situations and budgets.

**For patios:** Natural stone offers premium results, quality concrete pavers provide good value, exposed aggregate works for modern designs.

**For driveways:** Exposed aggregate concrete balances cost, durability, and appearance best for most homeowners. Permeable options suit properties needing stormwater management.

Don't over-build for the application - expensive driveway paving is unnecessary for a garden path. But don't under-build either - budget paving in high-use areas fails quickly.

Start with proper base preparation regardless of paving choice. The base determines longevity far more than the paving material itself.

And remember: paving is expensive to replace. Get it right once rather than cheaply twice. Quality materials and professional installation cost more upfront but deliver decades of trouble-free performance.

Your paving choices transform unusable ground into functional, attractive hardscape that serves daily for years to come. Choose thoughtfully.
